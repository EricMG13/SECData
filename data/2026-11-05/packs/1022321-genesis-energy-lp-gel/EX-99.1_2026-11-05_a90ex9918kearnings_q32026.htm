Genesis Energy, L.P. Reports Third Quarter 2026 Results

HOUSTON – (BUSINESS WIRE) – Genesis Energy, L.P. (NYSE: GEL) today announced its third quarter results.
We generated the following financial results for the third quarter of 2026:

•Net Income Attributable to Genesis Energy, L.P. of $2.4 million for the third quarter of 2026 compared to Net Income Attributable to Genesis Energy, L.P. of $9.2 million for the same period in 2025.

•Cash Flows from Operating Activities of $78.4 million for the third quarter of 2026 compared to $70.3 million for the same period in 2025.

•We declared cash distributions on our preferred units of $0.9473 for each preferred unit, which equates to a cash distribution of approximately $8.8 million and is reflected as a reduction to Available Cash before Reserves to common unitholders.

•Available Cash before Reserves to common unitholders of $38.6 million for the third quarter of 2026, which provided 1.58X coverage for the quarterly distribution of $0.20 per common unit attributable to the third quarter.

•Total Segment Margin of $147.2 million for the third quarter of 2026.
    
•Adjusted EBITDA of $130.5 million for the third quarter of 2026.

•Adjusted Consolidated EBITDA of $607.5 million for the trailing twelve months ended September 30, 2026 and a bank leverage ratio of 5.03X, both calculated in accordance with our senior secured credit agreement and discussed further in this release.

Grant Sims, CEO of Genesis Energy, said, “Our third quarter results came in modestly ahead of our internal expectations. Our Offshore Pipeline Transportation segment experienced no disruptions of throughput from weather-related producer shut-ins during what is typically the most active part of hurricane season. The benefit we would have otherwise expected to realize from such favorable weather conditions, however, was partially offset by lower than anticipated volumes from certain production facilities which flow exclusively through our pipeline transportation network. It is now expected that this lower than originally expected production from a couple of these deepwater facilities will continue into the fourth quarter and into the early part of 2027, until four new, high-impact wells are completed and turned to production. Our Onshore Transportation and Services segment once again performed ahead of our expectations. Our Marine Transportation segment was in line with our expectations, even though a significant dry-dock that started in the second quarter unexpectedly extended into the third quarter. Taken together, we are pleased with the quarter, and regardless of timing, we are encouraged by the level of activity around our irreplaceable midstream infrastructure in the Gulf of America as well as the macro fundamentals driving our other businesses.

Following the significant financing activity in the first half of the year, there is little to report on the balance sheet this quarter. We ended the quarter with no borrowings outstanding under our $900 million senior secured credit facility, approximately $41.5 million of unrestricted cash on hand, no debt maturities until 2029, and a bank-calculated leverage ratio of approximately 5.03x. We continue to focus on rightsizing and optimizing our capital structure, expecting to drive future cash savings to add to those already realized from the steps we took earlier this year.

Consistent with the ‘all-of-the-above’ approach to capital allocation that we have discussed prior to, and since, turning cash-flow positive, the board of directors declared a quarterly distribution of $0.20 per common unit, unchanged from last quarter but an increase of approximately 21% as compared to the third quarter of last year. Our capital allocation priorities remain unchanged as we expect to continue to retire our highest-cost capital, reduce our debt in absolute terms, and thoughtfully and prudently grow distributions to our common unitholders over time, while preserving our financial flexibility to pursue attractive organic and inorganic opportunities as they emerge in the future.

With that, I will briefly discuss our individual business segments in more detail.

Our Offshore Pipeline Transportation segment operated without any weather-related downtime during the quarter. As noted above, however, that benefit was partially offset by lower than anticipated volumes, including unplanned curtailments at a couple of our legacy connected fields, the continuing challenges from two of the initial wells at Shenandoah, and a planned turnaround at Salamanca that was moved forward from the fourth quarter into the third quarter. As we said last quarter, we do not believe any of these items have longer-term negative implications for the ultimate recovery of reserves already discovered and being developed.

We expect two new wells from the Monument field to be completed and flow across the Shenandoah facility and on to shore through our oil pipelines by the end of the first quarter of next year. We expect that rig to next drill and complete a fifth producing well at the Shenandoah field by the end of 2027. There are two rigs on location drilling wells to be completed and tied into the Salamanca facility, the production from which will flow on to shore through us.

We should point out that nine of the sixteen deepwater drilling units currently available and active in the Gulf of America are working on leases that are contractually dedicated to flow through our existing facilities, and the incremental production from which would require no additional capital from us to accommodate. The overriding constraint on the pace of production growth and therefore, throughput on our pipelines is the number of rigs capable of working in the deepwater Gulf lease area. There is no doubt the basin is world class and would be more active if suitable rigs were available.

In fact, there are two new rigs expected to enter service over the next several years. As we see it then, it is not a matter of if, it is really only a matter of when the upstream industry will be able to develop the known and to-be-discovered oil in place throughout the areas served by our critical and irreplaceable pipeline franchise. Whenever such drilling does occur, we will benefit from it, just as we continue to benefit from the producer activity undertaken over the last three decades.

During the third quarter, our Marine Transportation segment performed in line with our expectations, even though the dry-docking of one of our larger offshore units that began in the second quarter extended further into the third quarter than originally planned. That unit is now back in service and there are no major dry-dockings expected until 2027. We are experiencing, in essence, 100% utilization and negotiated spot rates for all classes of vessels are extremely robust, which will ultimately allow term contracts to follow higher. We see little on the horizon that could significantly affect this tight supply and demand for marine tonnage.

Our Onshore Transportation and Services segment once again performed ahead of our expectations. Certain of the opportunities that arose last quarter continued into the third quarter and are present in the fourth quarter. We now believe we could possibly benefit from such opportunities in 2027, and perhaps beyond, as some of the dislocations caused by the Iranian situation, as well as the resumption of significant imports from Venezuela, could take years to fully return to historic norms.

As we look toward the remainder of the year, we should point out we experienced approximately four days of service disruptions in our Offshore Pipeline Transportation segment due to Hurricane Isaias in early October. Additionally, a major legacy production facility was partially curtailed for the better part of a month because of its inability to transport associated natural gas production due to maintenance activities on the gas export pipeline leaving such facility. Such production facility has now resumed normal operations. As a result of these items, in addition to the now lower anticipated volumes discussed above, we will likely be near, or possibly even below, the low end of our original guidance for Adjusted EBITDA for the year. Having said that, we would reiterate that coming in at the low end of 2026 guidance, or missing the fourth quarter, has little to do with the underlying fundamentals and the ultimate cash flow generating capabilities of our businesses, especially in our largest segment.

Stepping back and keeping everything in focus, 2026 has indeed turned out to be a transformational year for Genesis, just as we anticipated. Our businesses, while not without third-party operational challenges relative to expectations at the beginning of the year, have performed well enough to allow us to retire roughly 40% of the remaining most expensive paper in

That is a solid year under almost any objective metrics, and we are proud to have delivered.

In closing, the management team and board of directors remain steadfast in our commitment to building long-term value for everyone in the capital structure, and we believe the decisions we are making reflect this commitment and our confidence in Genesis moving forward. I would once again like to thank our entire workforce for their continued dedication to safe, reliable, and responsible operations. I’m proud to have the opportunity to work alongside each and every one of you.”

Offshore pipeline transportation Segment Margin for the 2026 Quarter decreased $3.5 million, or 4%, from the 2025 Quarter primarily due to: (i) producer downtime at several legacy fields from planned and unplanned maintenance; and (ii) producer underperformance at several key fields attached to our pipeline infrastructure, including the Shenandoah floating production unit (“FPU”), which had operational challenges at several of its wells during the 2026 Quarter. As a result of these challenges at Shenandoah, volumes during the 2026 Quarter were below the minimum volume commitment (“MVC”) and we were only able to recognize our MVC in Segment Margin, whereas production volumes from Shenandoah exceeded the MVC in the 2025 Quarter. The production from all of these impacted fields and wells impacted our results as they are molecules that we touch multiple times throughout our oil and natural gas pipeline infrastructure. These decreases were partially offset by a full quarter of production volumes in the 2026 Quarter associated with the Salamanca FPU, which began producing in September 2025. The Salamanca FPU ties into our existing 100% owned SEKCO Pipeline for further transportation downstream on our 64% owned Poseidon Pipeline.
Marine transportation Segment Margin for the 2026 Quarter increased $3.1 million, or 12%, from the 2025 Quarter primarily due to: (i) an increase in fleet utilization in our inland and offshore barge businesses during the 2026 Quarter; and (ii) an increase in day rates in our inland barge business due to a recovery in rates from the 2025 Quarter that experienced a decline in Midwest refinery demand for black oil equipment as a result of changing crude slates to lighter oil. In addition, our M/T American Phoenix had a higher contractual rate during the 2026 Quarter compared to the 2025 Quarter.
Onshore transportation and services Segment Margin for the 2026 Quarter increased $1.1 million, or 5%, from the 2025 Quarter primarily due to an increase in volumes transported on our onshore crude oil pipeline systems. We experienced an increase in volumes on our Texas pipeline system, which is a key destination point for various grades of crude oil produced in the Gulf of America including those transported on our 64% owned CHOPS Pipeline. In our sulfur services business, we experienced an increase in Segment Margin in the 2026 Quarter primarily due to an increase in the index-based NaHS sales prices and an increase in NaHS sales volumes.
Other Components of Net Income
We reported Net Income Attributable to Genesis Energy, L.P. of $2.4 million in the 2026 Quarter compared to Net Income Attributable to Genesis Energy, L.P. of $9.2 million in the 2025 Quarter.
Net Income Attributable to Genesis Energy, L.P. in the 2026 Quarter was impacted by an increase in depreciation and amortization expense of $3.9 million and an increase in general and administrative expense of $3.1 million during the 2026 Quarter.

AVAILABLE CASH BEFORE RESERVES
Purposes, Uses and Definition
Available Cash before Reserves, often referred to by others as distributable cash flow, is a quantitative standard used throughout the investment community with respect to publicly traded partnerships and is commonly used as a supplemental financial measure by management and by external users of financial statements such as investors, commercial banks, research analysts and rating agencies, to aid in assessing, among other things:
(1)the financial performance of our assets;
(2)our operating performance;
(3)the viability of potential projects, including our cash and overall return on alternative capital investments as compared to those of other companies in the midstream energy industry;
(4)the ability of our assets to generate cash sufficient to satisfy certain non-discretionary cash requirements, including interest payments and certain maintenance capital requirements; and
(5)our ability to make certain discretionary payments, such as distributions on our preferred and common units, growth capital expenditures, certain maintenance capital expenditures and early payments of indebtedness.
We define Available Cash before Reserves (“Available Cash before Reserves”) as Adjusted EBITDA adjusted for certain items, the most significant of which in the relevant reporting periods have been the sum of maintenance capital utilized, interest expense, net, cash tax expense and cash distributions attributable to our Class A Convertible Preferred unitholders.
Disclosure Format Relating to Maintenance Capital
We use a modified format relating to maintenance capital requirements because our maintenance capital expenditures vary materially in nature (discretionary vs. non-discretionary), timing and amount from time to time. We believe that, without such modified disclosure, such changes in our maintenance capital expenditures could be confusing and potentially misleading to users of our financial information, particularly in the context of the nature and purposes of our Available Cash before Reserves measure. Our modified disclosure format provides those users with information in the form of our maintenance capital utilized measure (which we deduct to arrive at Available Cash before Reserves). Our maintenance capital utilized measure constitutes a proxy for non-discretionary maintenance capital expenditures and it takes into consideration the relationship among maintenance capital expenditures, operating expenses and depreciation from period to period.

Maintenance Capital Requirements
Maintenance Capital Expenditures
Maintenance capital expenditures are capitalized costs that are necessary to maintain the service capability of our existing assets, including the replacement of any system component or equipment which is worn out or obsolete. Maintenance capital expenditures can be discretionary or non-discretionary, depending on the facts and circumstances.
Prior to 2014, substantially all of our maintenance capital expenditures were (a) related to our pipeline assets and similar infrastructure, (b) non-discretionary in nature and (c) immaterial in amount as compared to our Available Cash before Reserves measure. Those historical expenditures were non-discretionary (or mandatory) in nature because we had very little (if any) discretion as to whether or when we incurred them. We had to incur them in order to continue to operate the related pipelines in a safe and reliable manner and consistently with past practices. If we had not made those expenditures, we would not have been able to continue to operate all or portions of those pipelines, which would not have been economically feasible. An example of a non-discretionary (or mandatory) maintenance capital expenditure would be replacing a segment of an old pipeline because one can no longer operate that pipeline safely, legally and/or economically in the absence of such replacement.
Beginning with 2014, we believe a substantial amount of our maintenance capital expenditures from time to time have been and will continue to be (a) related to our assets other than pipelines, such as our marine vessels, trucks and similar assets, (b) discretionary in nature and (c) potentially material in amount as compared to our Available Cash before Reserves measure. Those expenditures will be discretionary (or non-mandatory) in nature because we will have significant discretion as to whether or when we incur them. We will not be forced to incur them in order to continue to operate the related assets in a safe and reliable manner. If we chose not to make those expenditures, we would be able to continue to operate those assets economically, although in lieu of maintenance capital expenditures, we would incur increased operating expenses, including maintenance expenses. An example of a discretionary (or non-mandatory) maintenance capital expenditure would be replacing an older marine vessel with a new marine vessel with substantially similar specifications, even though one could continue to economically operate the older vessel in spite of its increasing maintenance and other operating expenses.
In summary, we are experiencing changes in the nature (discretionary vs. non-discretionary), timing and amount of our maintenance capital expenditures that merit a more detailed review and analysis than was required historically. Management’s increasing ability to determine if and when to incur certain maintenance capital expenditures is relevant to the manner in which we analyze aspects of our business relating to discretionary and non-discretionary expenditures. We believe it would be inappropriate to derive our Available Cash before Reserves measure by deducting discretionary maintenance capital expenditures, which we believe are similar in nature in this context to certain other discretionary expenditures, such as growth capital expenditures, distributions/dividends and equity buybacks. Unfortunately, not all maintenance capital expenditures are clearly discretionary or non-discretionary in nature. Therefore, we developed a measure, maintenance capital utilized, that we believe is more useful in the determination of Available Cash before Reserves.
Maintenance Capital Utilized
We believe our maintenance capital utilized measure is the most useful quarterly maintenance capital requirements measure to use to derive our Available Cash before Reserves measure. We define our maintenance capital utilized measure as that portion of the amount of previously incurred maintenance capital expenditures that we utilize during the relevant quarter, which would be equal to the sum of the maintenance capital expenditures we have incurred for each project/component in prior quarters allocated ratably over the useful lives of those projects/components.
Our maintenance capital utilized measure constitutes a proxy for non-discretionary maintenance capital expenditures and it takes into consideration the relationship among maintenance capital expenditures, operating expenses and depreciation from period to period. Because we did not initially use our maintenance capital utilized measure before 2014, our maintenance capital utilized calculations will reflect the utilization of solely those maintenance capital expenditures incurred since December 31, 2013.
